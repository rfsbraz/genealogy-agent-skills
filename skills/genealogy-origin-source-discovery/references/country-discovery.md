# Country discovery protocol

## Inputs and triggers

Use each person's evidenced birth, residence, parental origins, migration, citizenship and institutional affiliations. Keep uncertainty and source IDs on every geographic link. Trigger discovery when a new locality/period/country appears, an origin is corrected, a collection is exhausted, a custody route fails or an existing guide is stale.

## Build the country profile

1. Search native-language terms for national/regional archives, civil registration, genealogy research guides and the desired event/record. Discover the terminology before assuming English terms retrieve local holdings.
2. Establish the period's jurisdictions and creators, including predecessors and colonial/overseas administrations when evidence makes them relevant.
3. Consult official finding aids, directories, inventories and source guides. Use scholarly/community/commercial guides to find leads, then verify actual holdings with custodians.
4. Discover sources beyond archives: libraries and digitised newspapers; gazetteers/maps; cemetery records; institutional collections; family papers; relevant historical publications and existing trees as attributed leads.
5. Record known source creation dates, population coverage and survival gaps with citations. Mark unknown coverage rather than filling it with another country's assumptions.
6. Create a person-specific source portfolio and send custody/access verification to repository-discovery.

## Cross-border logic

Search origin and destination in a linked plan. A destination marriage, death, residence or citizenship file can name a more precise origin. A passport or departure record may identify family or destination. Test identity across both sides with names, relatives, occupation, age intervals, address and travel chronology. Dates reported long after migration may be approximate; nationality is not necessarily birthplace or ancestry.

When origin is unknown, prioritize destination records likely to identify it. Preserve competing origin candidates and label each search branch. Do not pursue every country suggested by a common surname.

## Portfolio entry

Use fields: person_id, question_id, origin_evidence, country, historical_jurisdiction, place_candidates, period, record_family, expected_information, collection_id/title, custodian, access_route, coverage_status, coverage_evidence, relevance_reason, priority_reason, limitations, last_checked, next_action.

Coverage status: verified_relevant, verified_not_relevant, candidate, inaccessible or unknown. A candidate source type is not a discovered collection.

## Stopping

Stop when useful next sources have been identified for the active question and remaining gaps are explicit, or when the bounded discovery budget is exhausted. Do not claim a country's sources are comprehensively mapped. Save the reusable country profile separately from private person data.
